Why Accreditation Matters

Differentiate Through International Standards

Parents in Tanzanian urban centres increasingly demand international curricula — Cambridge IGCSE, A-Levels, IB, and American systems — as pathways to overseas university admission and global career mobility.

Schools such as IST, Braeburn, HOPAC, Feza, and Aga Khan already offer international tracks, but dozens more aspire to transition. The gap between aspiration and readiness is precisely where OCBI creates value: providing the structured consulting, teacher retraining, and quality assurance infrastructure needed to achieve and maintain international accreditation.

Curriculum Pathways Decision Guide

Schools migrating to international systems face trade-offs between cost, complexity, and marketability. We guide you through each option.

NECTA

National Curriculum (NECTA)

Approval: Integrated into standard MoEST/SAS registration process.
Teachers: Must be registered. Focus on Competence-Based Curriculum (CBC) pedagogy.
Cost: Lower cost; aligned with national capitation grants and public funding.
Best For: Schools primarily serving the domestic market with government-aligned curricula.
CIE

Cambridge International

Approval: Application process with approval visit by Cambridge representatives. Stricter upfront gate.
Teachers: Require Cambridge Professional Development Qualifications (PDQs).
Cost: Application fee, annual programme fees, and learner registration fees apply.
Best For: Schools seeking strong brand recognition in East Africa and IGCSE/A-Level qualifications.
COG

Cognia (American System)

Approval: Continuous improvement model; less rigid upfront gate than Cambridge.
Teachers: Access to eleot (Effective Learning Environments Observation Tool) for assessment.
Cost: USD 3,000 annual membership (outside US) plus Engagement Review fees.
Best For: Schools wanting to signal quality quickly. Can market improvement before full accreditation.
IB

International Baccalaureate

Approval: Multi-stage candidacy and authorisation process. Most rigorous pathway.
Teachers: IB-specific training and workshops required for all programme teachers.
Cost: Candidacy fees, annual programme fees, and per-student examination fees.
Best For: Schools targeting globally mobile families seeking the highest international recognition.
Strategic Insight

Cognia vs Cambridge: A Strategic Choice

Cognia's continuous improvement framework can be marketed as a quality differentiator even before full accreditation is achieved, making it attractive for schools that want to signal quality quickly. Cambridge, by contrast, requires a stricter upfront approval visit but carries stronger brand recognition in East Africa.

OCBI's accreditation consultants help you make the right strategic choice based on your school's positioning, parent demographics, budget, and long-term vision. Many schools pursue a dual strategy — starting with Cognia for rapid quality signalling while preparing for Cambridge or IB as a longer-term goal.

Regulatory Framework

Mainland & Zanzibar Compliance

Operating across Tanzania requires navigating distinct regulatory bodies on the Mainland and in Zanzibar.

🏛

Mainland Tanzania

Sequential compliance with BRELA, TRA, NACTVET, MoEST, and PO-RALG. School Quality Control managed by offices in all 26 regions. CPD must align with the National Framework for Continuous Professional Development.

🏝

Zanzibar

Semi-autonomous status requires separate legal entity. Ministry of Education and Vocational Training (MoEVT) oversees education. Vocational Training Authority (VTA) regulates TVET under the Vocational Training Act (2006). Distinct labour laws apply.
